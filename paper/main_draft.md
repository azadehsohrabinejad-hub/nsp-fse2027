Neural State-Space Process (NSP): Modeling Semantic Drift in LLM-Based Program Repair Trajectories
Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used for autonomous program repair, typically evaluated by their ability to pass existing unit tests. However, this binary test-passing reward signal is fundamentally flawed: LLMs can overfit to visible tests while introducing severe semantic regressions—a phenomenon we term Semantic Drift. Existing evaluation methods lack the temporal and semantic depth to detect when an agent's repair trajectory diverges from the intended behavioral contract.

In this paper, we introduce the Neural State-Space Process (NSP), a novel framework that models LLM repair trajectories as dynamic latent systems. Rather than evaluating repairs statically, NSP infers a four-dimensional hidden state—comprising Repair Progress, Semantic Alignment, Context Quality, and Repair-Policy Stability—from heterogeneous round-level observations. We formally define Semantic Drift as a deceptive trajectory pattern where observable repair progress increases while latent semantic alignment deteriorates.

We provide a mathematically rigorous specification of the NSP observation model, incorporating non-Gaussian likelihoods tailored to the heterogeneous nature of repair artifacts. We implement and evaluate three estimators—Gradient-based MLE, Classical Expectation-Maximization (EM), and Particle Filtering—proving their convergence and parameter recovery on synthetic data. Applied to a canonical corpus of 1,184 CCBench repair trajectories, NSP demonstrates robust fit and cross-model consistency. Our results show that NSP provides a statistically sound, trajectory-level foundation for understanding and ultimately mitigating semantic degradation in autonomous repair agents.
